For iPhone, iPad and iPhone Duo. Free, with an optional one-time Premium. No account, no ads, no tracking.

Strike, Ball, Out

A code is three different digits, and it can start with 0. Secret 427, guess 472: one Strike, two Balls.

S

A right digit in the right place.

B

A right digit in the wrong place.

0

Out

None of the digits are in the code.

BattleTable

Lay an iPad flat between two players. Each gets half the screen, turned to face them, with their own keypad, board and history. Choose secrets in turn while the other half is covered, or both at once behind a folded card. Tap Hide whenever you like.

Five ways to crack it

  • Daily Code: one 4-digit code a day, the same for everyone, ten guesses. Share your result as coloured dots that never give the code away, and keep a run of days going.
  • Classic: the original. Crack the computer's code in as few guesses as you can.
  • Number Battle: you both choose a code, then race to crack each other's. You see your answer at once, and a round ends when you've both guessed, so going first never helps.
  • Fleet Battle: hide a galleon, a brig and a sloop on an 8×8 sea, then find theirs. Each shot shows at once: miss, hit or sunk. Once a match, sonar counts the ship squares in a 3×3 area.
  • The Mole: a party game for 3 to 8 players on their own devices. Crack a vault code together, while one of you, the Mole, quietly steers everyone wrong.

Every keypad has Notes for marking digits out, maybe or in, and any match can have a 15- or 30-second turn clock.

A fair opponent

The computer sees only what you would: its own guesses and their answers, or its own shots. It never peeks. Each opponent has its own personality, and a word or two to say across the table.

  • Rookie: guesses freely, and uses a little of what it learns.
  • Detective and Hunter: only guess codes that fit, or search the sea in a pattern.
  • Master and Admiral: pick the guess that rules out the most, or fire where a ship is likeliest.
  • Nearby: two players on two devices, no internet. Host a game, read out the code, and play; each device shows only its own side.

Questions

I played 3 Strikes on Apple Watch. What changed?

3 Strikes 2 is rebuilt from scratch as an update to the same app, now for iPhone and iPad. The original game is here as Classic, free, alongside the Daily Code, Number Battle, Fleet Battle, The Mole and BattleTable. An Apple Watch version may follow.

Can the other player see my code on BattleTable?

Not through the game: your code shows as dots until you touch and hold it, your half covers itself during setup and whenever the app leaves the screen, and you can tap Hide at any time. But one iPad can't promise privacy from someone sitting opposite, and VoiceOver and sounds play for both. A folded card across the middle helps.

How does Nearby keep my code secret?

It never leaves your device during the match. Each device sends a sealed commitment to its code or fleet, answers the other's guesses itself, and opens the seal only at the end, when every answer is checked. The connection is encrypted with the six-digit pairing code. It's made for friendly games: a false answer is caught at the end rather than prevented.

Does the computer cheat?

No. It's given only what a player in its seat would know, and every answer it gets is true. Harder levels just think better.

Does it need an internet connection, or an account?

Neither. Everything works offline, the Daily Code included: it's worked out from the date on your device. A connection is used only to buy or restore Premium.

Can two people play on one iPhone?

Yes: turn on Pass and Play in Settings, under Two Players, One Device. The screen hides after every turn until the next player taps I'm Ready.
