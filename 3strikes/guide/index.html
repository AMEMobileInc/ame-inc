Quick start

  1. Try Classic

    Home, Classic, Start. Type three different digits and tap Guess.

  2. Read the answer

    Strikes are right digits in the right place, Balls are right digits in the wrong place. Use them to narrow it down.

  3. Crack it

    Three Strikes and the code is yours. Then try Number Battle against the computer, or a friend.

Strike, Ball, Out

A code is three different digits, and it can start with 0, so 047 is a fine code. Every guess gets an answer:

AnswerMeansOn screen

BallA right digit in the wrong placeB, with gold lamps

Tip: an Out is great news. It rules out three digits at once, everywhere in the code.

Daily Code

One 4-digit code a day, the same for everyone, with ten guesses. Free. A new code arrives at your midnight.

  • Once a day: today's code can be played once. Come back tomorrow for the next number.
  • Share Result sends coloured dots, one row per guess: 🔴 a Strike, 🟡 a Ball, ⚪ neither. Never the digits, so nobody's spoiled.
  • Runs: Statistics counts the days you've cracked it in a row, and your best run.
  • Reminder: tap Remind Me Each Day, or Settings, Daily Code Reminder, and pick a time. It's skipped on days you've already played. Off unless you turn it on.

Classic

The original 3 Strikes from Apple Watch. The computer picks a code; crack it in as few guesses as you can.

  • Digits: 3 free; 4 and 5 with Premium.
  • Give Up shows the code. Your best for each length is kept in Statistics.
  • Hint: Settings, Show How Many Codes Still Fit, counts the codes that match every answer so far.
  • Close the app mid-game and it's still there next time.

Notes and the turn clock

  • Notes: tap Notes under the keypad, then tap a digit: once crosses it out, twice adds a dot for maybe, three times rings it as in the code, four times clears it. Tap Notes again to type. Marks stay on the keys and are cleared when the game ends.
  • Turn clock: when you start a match, choose 15 or 30 seconds a move. When time runs out, your guess goes in if it's complete, or a random one if not; at sea, your aimed shot or a random square.

Number Battle

You both choose a secret code, then race to crack each other's.

  1. Type your code, or tap Random Code, then Lock Code.
  2. Each round, type a guess and Lock Guess. Your answer shows straight away; the round ends when both players have guessed.
  3. The first to crack the other code wins. Both in the same round is a draw, so going first never helps.
  • Your guesses / Their guesses switches between your deductions and their guesses at your code.
  • Limit: with 10 or 15 rounds, nobody cracking it in time is a draw.
  • End stops a match without counting it. Going back keeps it to continue from Home.

Fleet Battle

Hide three ships on an 8×8 sea, A to H across and 1 to 8 down, then find theirs.

  • Your fleet: a galleon (4 squares), a brig (3) and a sloop (2), each in a straight line. They can touch, but not overlap.
  • Placing: you start with a random fleet. Tap a ship to pick it, tap the water to move it, and Turn to swing it round. Shuffle for a new layout, then Lock Fleet.
  • Firing: tap a square to aim, then Fire. Pick for Me chooses an untried square. You see where your shot landed straight away; the round ends when both have fired.
  • Marks: a dot is a miss, an orange × a hit, and a dark hull a sunk ship, shown whole.
  • Sonar: once a match, tap the radio-waves button, choose a square, then Ping. Instead of firing, it counts the ship squares in the 3×3 around it, hit or not, and shows the number there. The other player sees where you listened. A ping that finds nothing clears all nine squares.
  • Your fleet, the small map beside Fire, shows your ships and where they've fired. Theirs / Yours shows it full size.
  • Sounds: a blaster when anyone fires, then a boom, a splash, or a ship going down. They play in silent mode unless you turn that off in Settings.
  • Sink the whole fleet to win. Both fleets sinking in the same round is a draw.

Playing the computer

Three levels for each game. The computer sees only what you would, its own guesses and answers or its own shots, and every answer it gets is true. It never peeks.

The Mole (3 to 8 players)

A party game, each player on their own iPhone or iPad, on the same Wi-Fi or nearby. Free.

  1. One player opens The Mole and hosts; the others join with the code on the host's screen.
  2. Everyone gets a secret role card: touch and hold to see it. The crew each hold one true clue about the vault code. The Mole knows the code and holds a false clue.
  3. Crack the code together with Strikes and Balls, talking it over. Anyone can call a vote. Catch the Mole or crack the code and the crew wins; otherwise the Mole does.

Pass and Play

Two players, one iPhone or iPad. Turn it on in Settings, under Two Players, One Device, then choose Pass and Play when you start a match.

  1. The screen says Pass to Player 1. Player 1 taps I'm Ready, chooses a secret, then taps Hide and Pass.
  2. Player 2 does the same. Then you take turns guessing or firing, handing over after each turn.
  3. Nothing private shows until the right player taps I'm Ready, and leaving the app hides everything.

BattleTable (iPad)

Lay an iPad flat on a table between two players, one at each short end. Each gets half the screen, turned to face them. BattleTable is always portrait, so turning the iPad never moves anything. On iPhone Duo, it works when the Duo is open.

  1. Choose BattleTable when you start a match. By default, Player 1 chooses a secret while Player 2's half stays covered, then they swap.
  2. Or turn on Both at once, behind a divider: stand something like a folded card across the middle first. The clear band in the middle is there for it, and its width is in Settings.
  3. Both players tap I'm Ready, then play at the same time, each in their own half.
  • Hide covers your half. Pause offers Resume, Restart and Leave. Both are along your own edge of the screen.
  • Your code shows as dots; touch and hold it to peek.
  • One iPad can't promise privacy: a glance across the table, VoiceOver or a sound can give something away.

Nearby (two devices)

Each player on their own iPhone or iPad, seeing only their own side. No internet or account: the devices talk directly over Wi-Fi.

  1. Host: Home, Nearby, Host Game. Choose the game and rules. A six-digit pairing code appears.
  2. Guest: Home, Nearby, Find Games. Tap the host's name and type their code.
  3. The host taps Accept, the guest taps I'm In, and the host taps Start Match.
  4. Play as usual: you see your own result as soon as you lock, and the round ends when you've both played. At the end, Play Again (host) starts another through the same steps.
  • Your secret stays on your device. Each device sends a sealed commitment, answers the other's guesses itself, and opens the seal at the end, when every answer is checked: "Every answer checked out".
  • Connection lost? The match waits at Reconnecting. If it can't reconnect, choose Retry, Save and Exit (continue later from Home), or End Match. Nobody wins by disconnecting.
  • Premium: 3-digit Number Battle is free. Fleet Battle and longer codes need the host to have Premium; guests join free.

Fixes

Find Games doesn't show the host

Check that the host is on the Host Game screen, both devices have Wi-Fi on, and both are close together. Being on the same Wi-Fi network is most reliable. Then check Local Network, below.

"Local Network is off for 3 Strikes"

Open Settings, then Privacy & Security, then Local Network, and turn on 3 Strikes on both devices.

"That code didn't match"

Type the six digits shown on the host's screen. Each time someone starts hosting, the code changes.

The match says Reconnecting

Keep both devices nearby with the app open. It carries on by itself when they find each other again, within 30 seconds. If not, tap Retry, or Save and Exit and continue later from Home on both devices.

My match disappeared

An unfinished match is kept until you start another match. Look for Continue at the top of Home. Classic keeps its own game separately.

Premium didn't unlock

Open Settings, then 3 Strikes Premium, then Restore Purchases, signed in with the Apple Account you bought it with.
