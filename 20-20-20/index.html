Set up in two minutes

A few choices at the start decide how well the habit fits your day. Do these once, and the app does the remembering.

  1. Allow notifications

    Reminders are how the habit starts. Allow them when 20.20.20 first opens.

    Missed it? Open the iPhone Settings app, then Notifications, then 20.20.20, and turn on Allow Notifications.

  2. Match reminders to your real working hours

    On Home, open Schedule Reminders and start from Work Hours (weekdays, 9 to 5), Extended Work (weekdays, 8 to 6) or Every Day (9 to 9), then adjust the days and times. Reminders outside the hours you use a screen just get muted, so keep the window tight.

  3. Keep the 20-minute rhythm to begin with

    Twenty minutes is the rule, and it is the default. With Plus, you can choose 30, 40 or 60 minutes. A longer interval you actually keep beats a shorter one you mute.

  4. Install the Apple Watch app

    On iPhone, open the Watch app, find 20.20.20 under Available Apps and tap Install. With your iPhone locked and your Watch on, reminders arrive on your wrist, so your phone can stay face down.

  5. Leave Background Haptics on

    In the 20.20.20 Watch app, tap the gear. Background Haptics keeps the countdown and the taps going after you lower your wrist. You feel each second pass while you look away, and you never need to check the screen.

  6. Place your "far point"

    Pick one spot at least 20 feet (6 metres) away that you can see from your desk: a window, the end of a hallway, a tree outside. Deciding it now means each break starts the moment the reminder arrives.

How to take a break that counts

Twenty seconds is short. These steps make every one of them rest your eyes rather than move them to another screen.

  1. Start straight from the reminder

    Tap the notification, or Start Now on your Watch, and the countdown begins at once. No menus, no extra taps.

  2. Put the screen down and look at your far point

    Look out at that spot for the whole 20 seconds. Let your eyes relax into the distance instead of reading something.

  3. Feel the time instead of watching it

    A light tap each second, on your wrist or your iPhone, tells you the break is running. A different tap tells you it's done.

  4. Blink slowly a few times

    Screen time tends to reduce blinking. A few slow, full blinks during the break help your eyes feel more comfortable.

  5. Not a good moment? Push it, don't skip it

    On Apple Watch, tap 5 min later. The reminder comes back in five minutes, so the break moves instead of disappearing.

Keeps going, wrist down

Each Watch break runs as a short Mind and Body workout of about 20 seconds, so the taps don't stop when the screen sleeps. Nothing is saved to Apple Health.

Your choice

Allow Apple Health when asked, so the Watch can keep running. Prefer iPhone only? Turn off Breaks on Apple Watch in Settings.

The Watch needs to be on your wrist and unlocked. A Watch that is locked, or charging, can't be opened from iPhone, so the break runs on iPhone only.

20.20.20 through your day

The same habit, set up for how you actually spend your time in front of a screen.

Desk work 9 to 5

Use Work Hours for your weekdays.

  • Keep your Watch on, and take breaks from your wrist while the phone stays in your bag.
  • Use a window as your far point.
  • Back-to-back calls? Tap 5 min later rather than muting.

Long focus sessions coding, writing, design

Deep focus is when people forget to look up.

  • Let the Watch tap you through the break so you don't lose your place on screen.
  • If 20 minutes breaks your flow, Plus lets you try 30 or 40 minutes. A rhythm you keep matters most.

Study and reading evenings, exams

Use Every Day (9 AM to 9 PM), or stretch the end time to cover your evening sessions.

  • Pick a far point in your study room before you start: a window or a doorway across the room.
  • Your Day Streak shows whether the habit held during a heavy week.

Meetings, travel, days off when you can't

Busy days happen, so make them quiet without losing the habit.

  • Mute for today, from Home, your Watch or the notification itself, and reminders start again tomorrow.
  • With Plus, pause for an hour or more instead.
  • Choose only the days you work, so weekends stay quiet.

Make it stick

Small changes that make the habit easier to keep for weeks, not just days.

Aim for your daily goal

Your goal matches the reminders in your schedule. Today and Day Streak on Home show how you're doing at a glance.

Finish, don't abandon

If you must stop early, tap Complete rather than leaving it. The time you actually spent is recorded.

Review your week

With Plus, open Progress Tracking each Friday and look at this week and your last 30 days. Days with fewer breaks usually show where a schedule change would help.

Questions

Why didn't my Apple Watch tap?

Check that Background Haptics is on: in the 20.20.20 Watch app, tap the gear. Reminders appear on the Watch when your iPhone is locked and your Watch is on your wrist. A locked or charging Watch can't run a break.

Do reminders need an internet connection?

Yes. Reminders are sent by our reminder service through Apple, so your iPhone needs a connection. Breaks, the timer and your progress all work without one.

Does it work without an Apple Watch?

Yes. Everything works on iPhone alone, with haptics each second and a calm countdown on screen.

Is this a medical app?

No. 20.20.20 is a wellness habit tool. It doesn't diagnose or treat any condition. If you have concerns about your eyes, please see an eye-care professional.
