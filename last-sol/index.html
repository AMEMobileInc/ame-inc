Last Sol: Mars ExpeditionLand, Explore, Survive, Return

Every landing changes your story.

It's 2030. Your lander is falling toward Mars, the fuel is low and the pad is small. Fly it down yourself, build a base out of whatever survived, and find a way home before the supplies run out. Or don't go home at all.

Coming soon to the App Store

For iPhone, iPad and Mac. Free, with an optional one-time Plus. No account, no ads, no tracking. Plays offline.

One sol at a time

A sol is a Mars day. Each one, you spend your energy on what matters most.

  1. Fly the landing

    Real Mars gravity, a parachute question and three pads to choose from. Hold to burn, nudge with the side jets. How well you land decides your supplies, your rover and your rocket.

  2. Build the base

    Solar arrays, habitats, greenhouses, a water extractor, a fuel plant, a reactor and more, placed on a map you walk around, a shift of work at a time.

  3. Drive and explore

    Take the rover to the ice crater, the mineral ridge and the silent rover that came before you. Explore on foot for landmarks, clues and things to make.

  4. Rest, and watch it work

    At night the buildings do their work: water, food, fuel and science, if the power holds. Then a new sol, and sometimes a new problem.

A base that really works

Build it, then watch it run.

  • Day and night power: solar by day, batteries and a reactor through the night, when the buildings do their work.
  • Build more: up to four of each building, placed anywhere open, with drones hauling ice, food and samples between them.
  • Mars fights back: dust storms dim the sun for days and machines break in the cold. Make repair kits and decide what to fix first.
  • Items and a recipe book: finds from every drive become rover upgrades, base gadgets and keepsakes.
  • A shuttle: build a hangar and fly to the other landing sites.

Real science, a robot at your side

The game is made up; the science isn't.

NASA souvenirs

Collect 22 real NASA photos of Mars as postcards, each with its credit, from Viking to Perseverance.

Mission Decisions

Real Mars problems with real answers, from MOXIE making oxygen to dust devils cleaning solar panels, kept in a Science Journal.

Earth, at light speed

Ask Mission Control a question and wait the real light time for the answer, minutes each way. It arrives as a notification.

Your robot

Minimus beeps, Frostbyte pings, Digby talks. It explains every building, points to the next step and helps on the build.

Mysteries to solve

Twelve clues in four mysteries, from an older, wetter Mars to someone who came before you.

A daily expedition

The same Mars for everyone today, with a twist of the day, on its own Game Center leaderboard.

Questions

Which devices does it run on?

iPhone and iPad on iOS 17 or later, including the iPhone Duo, and Mac. Buy Plus once and it unlocks on all of them with the same Apple Account.

Does it need an internet connection, or an account?

Neither. Everything plays offline. Game Center is optional and used only for the leaderboards, and a connection is used only to buy or restore Plus.

Are the photos real?

Yes. The souvenirs are real NASA images, each shown with its credit, such as NASA/JPL-Caltech or NASA/JPL/University of Arizona. NASA does not endorse this game.

Why does it ask to send notifications?

Only to tell you an answer from Earth has arrived, after the real light time between the planets. The notification is scheduled on your device; nothing is sent to us.

I bought Plus on another device. How do I get it back?

Open Plus from the title screen and tap Restore, signed in with the same Apple Account.
